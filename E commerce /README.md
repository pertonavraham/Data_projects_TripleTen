# E-Commerce Conversion & Retention Analysis

This project provides a comprehensive audit of user behavior for an e-commerce platform, focusing on the conversion funnel from initial view to final purchase, as well as long-term customer retention through cohort analysis.

## Project Overview
The primary objective of this analysis is to identify friction points in the user journey and evaluate the sustainability of customer acquisition. By segmenting data into functional areas such as purchase activity and first-purchase dates, the project offers a data-driven look at how seasonal trends (like the holiday season) impact long-term retention.

## Key Features
*   **Conversion Funnel Modeling:** Detailed tracking of users moving from 'View' (10,453 unique users) to 'Shopping Cart' (29%) and final 'Purchase' (10%).
*   **Cohort Analysis:** A week-over-week retention matrix that identifies how specific user groups (e.g., the Sept 2020 cohort) decay over a 4-week period.
*   **Executive Synopsis:** Strategic summary highlighting that while only 10% of total viewers convert, those who reach the 'Shopping Cart' stage show a significantly higher conversion probability (36%).
*   **Granular Purchase Activity:** Log of individual transactions across categories like computers, stationery, and appliances to identify high-performing brands like **Logitech** and **Samsung**.

## Tech Stack & Tools
*   **Excel/Google Sheets:** The primary engine for data cleaning, pivot table generation, and retention calculations.
*   **Data Architecture:** Utilization of seven distinct sheets—including `raw_user_activity`, `conversion_funnel`, and `retention_rates`—to maintain a structured analysis pipeline.

## Analysis Summary
The data reveals a critical drop-off point between initial browsing and carting, yet shows a strong 36% conversion rate for users who engage with the shopping cart. Cohort data from late 2020 indicates that holiday-season acquisitions often resulted in "one-time" shoppers, with retention rates for the September cohort dropping from 12.5% in week one to 3.13% by week four. These insights suggest a need for improved re-engagement strategies following peak sales periods.
